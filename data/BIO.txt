Genome indexing structures such as FM-indexes and k-mer indexes speed up read search and alignment by mapping short sequence substrings to reference positions.

DNA sequencing determines nucleotide order along strands, producing reads that require assembly, error correction, and alignment against reference sequences.

Sequence alignment scores nucleotide or protein matches, gaps, and substitutions to measure sequence similarity, homology, and evolutionary relationships.

K-mer indexing and hashing break biological sequences into fixed-length substrings so approximate matching and lookup filter candidate hits rapidly.

RNA sequencing measures gene expression and transcript isoform abundance across samples, requiring read alignment, counting, and normalization.

Phylogenetic trees represent evolutionary branching and sequence divergence among species or gene families inferred from aligned molecular sequences.

Metabolic network and pathway analysis models reaction graphs and flux balance to understand cellular metabolism and regulation.

Motif search and pattern matching scan biological sequences for conserved regulatory or binding motifs using position weight matrices and index structures.

De novo sequence assembly reconstructs longer contiguous sequences from overlapping reads using de Bruijn graphs and sequence overlap paths.

Variant calling identifies single-nucleotide changes and indels from aligned sequencing reads compared against a reference genome.

Protein structure comparison and alignment evaluate 3D folding similarity and conserved domains across related biological sequences.

Single-cell sequencing profiles expression across individual cells, using dimensionality reduction and nearest-neighbor graphs to cluster cell states.
