Reference genomes are preprocessed into compact substring indexes so short reads can be located quickly without full scans.

Next-generation platforms produce short fragments from many molecules in parallel and rely on downstream processing to recover order.

Global and local alignment algorithms score matches, mismatches, and affine gaps to quantify how closely two biological sequences correspond.

Short subsequence hashing of reads allows seed-and-extend aligners to narrow candidate loci before doing full dynamic programming.

Expression profiling by sequencing counts reads per transcript and normalises for length and library size to estimate abundance.

Distance-based and character-based trees group taxa by shared derived traits inferred from aligned molecular characters.

Reaction networks encode stoichiometry and constraints so flux distributions consistent with cellular physiology can be computed.

Conserved sequence patterns are detected by scanning with position-specific weight matrices and statistical thresholds.

Overlap-layout-consensus pipelines chain reads by their overlaps and resolve conflicts to build longer contiguous sequences.

Aligned reads are scanned for mismatches and small indels relative to a reference to identify variants.

Fold comparison aligns three-dimensional coordinates of related proteins to identify shared structural cores.

Cell-by-cell expression matrices are reduced to low-dimensional representations and clustered to reveal distinct cell populations.
